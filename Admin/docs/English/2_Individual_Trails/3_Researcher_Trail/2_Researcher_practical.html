The term transdisciplinarity is characterized by a wide variety of nuanced definitions.

 In the TRAIL, we use the definition from the OECD (2021):

"the integration of academic researchers from different disciplines with non-academic partners to jointly create new knowledge and theories in pursuit of a common goal."

This definition highlights three essential features that distinguish transdisciplinarity from other forms of collaboration:

  1. Type of collaboration partners: Academic and non-academic partners learn, work, and conduct research together in transdisciplinary settings.
  2. Nature of the problem addressed: The focus is on complex societal issues without clear-cut solutions, often referred to as "wicked problems."
  3. Nature of involvement: Different partners learn and work on an equal footing, genuinely collaborating to generate new knowledge and theories.

This TRAIL has a special focus on transdisciplinary education.

At TRAIL, we define transdisciplinary education as:

"the integration of researchers, teachers, and students from different disciplines with non-academic participants who co-create insights, new knowledge, or products to achieve a common goal."

In transdisciplinary education, teachers and students are also considered collaboration partners. This differs from transdisciplinary research, which primarily involves researchers from various disciplines working with non-academic partners to create new knowledge and innovations. TRAIL aims to serve all interests equally. At the same time, the inclusion of students presents challenges in realizing this equality, due to differing interests and power dynamics, which will be further discussed in the base camp.

Inter- or transdisciplinary: common confusions

Academic literature and dialogues with experts emphasize the lack of a shared understanding of transdisciplinarity (Klein, 2018).

 In particular, the distinction from interdisciplinary collaboration is not always clear. While both approaches aim to integrate different types of knowledge, transdisciplinarity is distinguished by the involvement of non-academic stakeholders as equal participants in the same project. In transdisciplinarity, the diversity of perspectives and "types of knowledge" is greater, and the problems addressed are generally more complex.
