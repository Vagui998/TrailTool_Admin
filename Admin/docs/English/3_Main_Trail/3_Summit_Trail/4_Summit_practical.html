Multiple types of outcomes—both visible and invisible—must evolve to create transformation. Since projects are usually relatively short (ranging from a week to ten weeks, or maybe a year), it’s important to consider sustainable ways to use, preserve, and further develop the knowledge, products, or skills gathered. 

Sustainable Relationships 

One key factor is maintaining sustainable relationships. For example, if a project takes place in a neighborhood involving students, researchers, and residents, it can be harmful to enter and leave the area in a short time. A solution could be to have a teacher, researcher, or facilitator work with the neighborhood over a longer period This allows for a deeper understanding of the area and helps build trust with residents. The need for longer-term relationships may also exist between participants, such as between students and societal partners. The latter may decide to collaborate with the former over the long term. 

Relay Learning 

To avoid superficial outcomes based on short-term study or collaboration, there is the option of relay learning. This means that a transdisciplinary team continues working on the outcome delivered by a previous team. This offers the opportunity to understand, define, or address the complex problem in a more grounded way. It’s also a form of collaboration between different teams—though not simultaneously. When choosing this model, it’s important to carefully transfer the information, knowledge, or actions previously created.
