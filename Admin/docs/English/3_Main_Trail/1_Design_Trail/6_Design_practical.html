Here are three key decisions to make when developing transdisciplinary education in higher education:


Intra- or Extracurricular 

A major decision is whether the educational program is extracurricular or intracurricular. 

  • Extracurricular options include summer or winter schools, which are short, intensive courses held outside the academic year. Another example is an honors program that runs alongside regular classes throughout the year to broaden the learning experience. 

  • Intracurricular options include regular classes, modules, minors, or a graduation project. These can be part of mandatory education or elective courses. 


Mandatory or Elective 

Another choice is whether the program is mandatory or offered as an elective. 

  • Choosing an elective has the advantage of attracting more motivated students and participants, which fosters active engagement from researchers, students, teachers, and societal partners. Setting admission requirements can further help attract the desired student profile. 

  • Since transdisciplinary education may be beneficial for all students, it can also be part of a mandatory course or program, encouraging all students to collaborate and explore different perspectives. 

 

Bachelor or Master 

It’s also important to determine whether transdisciplinary education takes place at the bachelor or master level. 

 Different motivations apply here: 

  • In a bachelor program, transdisciplinary education offers students the opportunity to develop a broad perspective and foundational skills early in their studies. 

  • In a master program, students can expand their expertise and focus on highly complex problems. 

 Bachelor-level education will be more introductory and exploratory, while master-level content will be more complex and in-depth. The choice to include transdisciplinary education in a bachelor or master program depends on the goals of the program, the expected learning outcomes, and the academic and professional readiness of the students. 

 

Scheduling 

Finally, it’s essential that the chosen format aligns with the preferences of participants — including students, researchers, teachers, and societal partners. It’s also important to consider whether all participants are present at the same time. 

  • Shorter periods with all participants present promote a more equitable learning community. 

  • Longer periods allow for a deeper exploration of multiple competencies.
