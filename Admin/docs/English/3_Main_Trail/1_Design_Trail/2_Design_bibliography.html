Transdisciplinary education requires different learning activities and teaching methods than you might be used to. Learning takes place through collaboration — with society, with other disciplines, and with yourself. 

A key feature of transdisciplinary education is the integration of different perspectives: scientific, professional, experiential, and societal insights are brought together. This integration process happens through co-creation — in collaboration between students and other stakeholders. 

This is referred to as collaborative integration. Students contribute their own knowledge and backgrounds — for example, from their studies or personal experiences — and work together to develop shared insights, solutions, or products (Oudenampsen et al., 2024). 

 

Five Integrative Educational Methods 

(Lambalgen & van der Tuin, 2024) 

To consciously guide collaborative integration, you can use integrative educational methods. These are teaching strategies that help students connect different perspectives together. 


Methode 

Focus van integratie 

Interdisciplinary Research Process  

Disciplinaire insights via common ground 

Travelling Concepts 

Different conceptual lenses 

Design Thinking 

Problem- and solution-oriented integration 

Object Orientation 

Analysis & reintegration of an object 

Systems Thinking 

System levels and interactions 

 

 Each method supports different learning outcomes. Choose what fits your learning objectives, context, and student group.
